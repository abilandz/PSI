**04.02.2026 Claudia Manunza, Giulia Pruna, Lukas Lautner:** _"ALICE 3 Outer Tracker Cooling Studies"_ 

**Abstract:** The ALICE collaboration has proposed a completely new apparatus, ALICE 3, for the LHC Run 5. The detector consists of a large tracking system based on Monolithic Active Pixel Sensors (MAPS), complemented by multiple systems for particle identification. The tracking system is divided into Inner and Outer Tracker. With 32m2, just the four barrel layers of the Outer Tracker will constitute the largest MAPS-based detector ever built and will require modularization and industrialization of its production. The about 45000 individual sensors will combined consume more than 10 kW and require dedicated cooling. Keeping modularity in mind, air cooling is the preferred option. Ongoing R&D work is focused on identifying the optimal cooling solution for the Outer Tracker. For this purpose, a cooling setup was build and recently upgraded at TUM. To adequately simulate the heat load dummy heater modules are employed and continuously developed to more closely match operational conditions.

We report on the upgrade of the setup, studied cooling methods and present first results showing the feasibility of air cooling the Outer Tracker.

**28.01.2026 Maximilian Korwieser:** _"Fast Femtoscopic Source Modeling with Machine Learning: From Collisions to Cosmic Rays"_ 

**Abstract:** Femtoscopic correlation measurements provide direct access to the space–time structure of particle emission in high-energy collisions. However, quantitative source studies are often limited by the high computational cost of evaluating realistic source models, making large-scale scans and systematic studies impractical. In this talk, I present a machine-learning surrogate model for femtoscopic sources that accurately emulates the full model calculation while reducing the evaluation time to effectively constant cost. Once trained, the surrogate enables fast and robust fits to arbitrarily large sets of correlation functions, opening new possibilities for high-statistics and multi-system analyses. Beyond acceleration, I discuss a task-transfer application: using the surrogate trained on heavy-ion data to estimate femtoscopic sources in very low-multiplicity and low-energy environments, where direct femtoscopic measurements are scarce. Machine learning is ideally suited to provide data-driven estimates in this regime. Such systems are relevant for understanding (anti-)nuclei production in cosmic rays, providing essential input for indirect dark matter searches by AMS and GAPS.

**21.01.2026 Rafael Manhart:** _"Results of the latest femtoscopic studies involving pions by ALICE in Run 3 & the connection to NS and axions"_ 

**Abstract:** The composition of the innermost region of neutron stars remains unknown, and the potential presence of QCD axions has been recently suggested. The properties of axions under high baryonic densities can be related to the in medium characteristics of pions, which can be studied in pp collisions at the LHC. The emission of multiple hadrons helps mimic high densities because their production distances are short (∼ fm). This talk presents the latest ALICE Run3 measurements of the pion-proton interaction using femtoscopy in pp collisions at √s = 13.6 TeV. Using three-body femtoscopy, we report the first measurement of p-p-pi correlations in the ongoing Run3 data-taking period, enabling the study of effects beyond pairwise interactions. This offers a distinctive opportunity to study axions and their impact on the equation of state of neutron stars.

**07.01.2025 Georgios Mantzaridis:** _"Probing the strangeness -2 sector with femtoscopy in ALICE with Run 3 data"_ 

**Abstract:** The interaction between nucleons and hyperons in the double-strange sector play a critical role in determining the equation of state of dense baryonic matter, with implications for the structure of neutron stars. Traditionally, this sector has been studied by using hypernuclear spectroscopy. However, the scarce data on double strange hypernuclei have so far limited the extraction of precise information on the ΞN interaction and the nature of the hyothetical (quasi)boundstate in the ΛΛ system is still not fully setteled.

During the LHC Run 2 data taking, the ALICE Collaboration has provided rich information on the low-energy features of the Λ-Λand p–Ξ− interactions by using the femtoscopy technique. The results were able to constrain (but not close) the parameterspace for the boundstate nature of in the ΛΛ and benchmark for the first time Lattice QCD calculations in the p–Ξ− system, providing experimental evidence of the attractive nature of the ΞN strong interaction. With the start of the LHC Run 3 data taking in 2022, ALICE has begun collecting significantly larger datasets, providing evidence of a pronounced signal at the opening of the Λ–Σ0 channel in the p–Ξ− correlation function. In this seminar we will discuss the current femtoscopic results in the strangeness -2 sector and present the preliminary result on p–Ξ− correlations measured in pp collisions from the first three years of the LHC Run 3. The measurement will be discussed by comparing the data to the correlation function calculated using the HAL-QCD potentials. Future prospects for extending these studies to the ΛΛ system will also be discussed.

**17.12.2025 Marta Krawczyk:** _"Study of Monolithic Active Pixel Sensors for ALICE 3"_ 

**Abstract:** The ALICE collaboration plans to replace its experimental apparatus for the LHC Run 5 by an entirely new one, called ALICE 3. A key element of ALICE 3 will be the Vertex Detector (VD). To achieve the optimal pointing resolution, the detector will combine a 10 μm pixel pitch and a small distance of only 5 mm to the interaction point with minimal material budget. Furthermore, due to the proximity to the beam pipe as well as the expected interaction rate of about 25 MHz in proton-proton collisions, the pixel chips of the VD will need to resist a radiation load of approximately 10^16 1 MeV n_eq/cm^2 of Non-Ionizing Energy Loss (NIEL) and approximately 300 Mrad of Total-Ionizing Dose (TID). Moreover, a time resolution of 100 ns is targeted. These requirements constitute a significant challenge to CMOS pixel sensors planned to be employed for the VD.

This seminar talk describes the recently started pixel chip characterization work at the expected radiation levels and as a function of temperature using the Analog Pixel Test Structure (APTS). The key items of this presentation will be the temperature and radiation dependence of the gain and the operation range of the APTS circuitry.

**19.11.2025 Maximilian Mahlein:** _"First observation of (anti)deuteron formation from resonance-decay nucleons"_ 

**Abstract:** The microscopic mechanism responsible for the formation of light (anti)(hyper)nuclei in hadron–hadron collisions remains one of the open questions in high-energy nuclear physics. While statistical hadronization and coalescence models both reproduce the measured yields, momentum spectra, and fluctuations in pp, p–A, and A–A collisions at ultra-relativistic energies, their fundamental physical assumptions are different, leading to model-dependent interpretations of the data.

In this work, new femtoscopy results on pion–deuteron correlations in pp collisions at √s = 13 TeV, measured by the ALICE experiment, are presented. The correlation function directly probes the residual interaction between pions and nucleons originating from short-lived Δ decays, followed by their coalescence into deuterons. This measurement provides, for the first time, a model-independent demonstration that (anti)deuteron production in ultra-relativistic pp collisions proceeds via nucleon coalescence. The results offer new constraints for the modeling of light- and heavy-nuclei formation in cosmic ray interactions and dark matter decay scenarios.

**12.11.2025 Seyed Farid Taghavi, Sebastian Wind:** _"Collective Dynamics in OO and pp Collision Systems"_ 

**Abstract:** In the first part, we discuss how collective behavior in high-energy collisions can be understood using a kinetic quasiparticle approach based on the Boltzmann equation. We introduce CoMBolt-ITA, a new tool for simulating relativistic systems in the relaxation time approximation, and apply it to recent OO collision data at the LHC to assess whether the system behaves more fluid-like or particle-like. In the second part, we compare source size predictions in pp collisions for free-streaming and ideal hydrodynamic evolution to highlight how spatial correlations reveal the degree of collectivity in small systems.

**29.10.2025 Wenya Wu :** _"Flow in small systems"_ 

**Abstract:** Quantum Chromodynamics predicts a transition from hadronic matter to a quark–gluon plasma (QGP) at extreme temperature and energy density, where quarks and gluons become deconfined. The QGP can be produced in high-energy heavy-ion collisions (HICs). Anisotropic flow coefficients, which characterize the collective motion of the medium, serve as key signatures of QGP formation. Measurements of transverse differential anisotropic flow reveal a baryon–meson grouping at intermediate transverse momentum, confirming QGP formation in HICs. Surprisingly, similar QGP-like features have been observed in high-multiplicity proton–proton and proton–lead collisions at the Large Hadron Collider (LHC). The ALICE Collaboration has reported, for the first time, a clear baryon–meson flow grouping in these small systems. Hydrodynamic models with quark coalescence successfully describe these results, suggesting that collective partonic behavior emerge in small collision systems.
