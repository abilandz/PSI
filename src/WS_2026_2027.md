**08.07.2026**

#### Mrunmoy Jena <a name="mrunmoy.jena"></a>

!!! note "Title: **_"Spectroscopy of ²⁴O and ²²O via ²⁵F(p,2p) quasi-free scattering at R3B Experiment"_"**"

    ???+ abstract
        Quasi-free proton and neutron-knockout reactions in inverse kinematics offer valuable insight into the structure of nuclei far from stability, and the kinematically complete R3B setup (Reactions with Relativistic Radioactive Beams) is ideally suited to study these reactions. The ²⁵F(p,2p)²⁴O reaction probes how a single proton extends the neutron drip line from Z=8, where it ends at ²⁴O, to Z=9 — where the ground-state overlap of ²⁵F with ²⁴O turns out to be surprisingly small, and its structural origin remains debated. Since the excited states of ²⁴O are neutron-unbound, they are reconstructed through their ²³O + n and ²²O + 2n decay channels.
    
        This talk presents the ongoing analysis, focusing on energy reconstruction with the CALIFA calorimeter and, in particular, on the ²²O + 2n + γ channel. From Doppler-corrected γ-ray spectra, the populations of the low-lying ²²O states are extracted using simulated response functions, and a γ-multiplicity analysis is developed to separate direct population of the 2⁺ state from cascade feeding.

**01.07.2026** 

#### Lena Kirchner, Anil Adigüzel <a name="lena.anil"></a>

!!! note "Title: **_"(baby)HYPER"_**"

	???+ abstract
		HYPER (Single-λ Hypernuclei Factory) is a pioneering experiment at CERN’s Antimatter Factory designed to produce and discover approximately 200 new single-λ hypernuclei using low-energy antiprotons from the ELENA ring. By capturing antiprotons onto nuclei, HYPER aims to perform high-resolution spectroscopy to measure ground-state binding energies and lifetimes, providing critical data to constrain λ-nucleon and λNN interactions and address the "hyperon puzzle" in neutron star physics. The experiment relies on a compact, multi-purpose detector &mdash; utilizing advanced ALICE ITS3 65 nm CMOS technology for its pion tracker &mdash; capable of distinguishing weak-decay events from prompt annihilation backgrounds through picosecond timing.
	    
	    The second part of the talk is about babyHYPER, a dedicated testbed operated during a test beam campaign at the TELMAX beamline at AD. It was designed to prototype key technical solutions for HYPER and to study particle tracking with MAPS detectors in a realistic setup. The testbed consists of a vacuum vessel with vacuum separation and two detector arms, each equipped with three sensors. This configuration allows the performance of the cooling and vacuum systems to be investigated while providing tracking information for vertex reconstruction at the target. The presentation will discuss the vessel design, the role of vacuum separation, and results from sensor cooling, vacuum performance, and tracking analysis.
