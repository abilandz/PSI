#### 21.10 - Marta Sailer

!!! note "Title: **_"Radiation-induced leakage and operating-temperature limits in APTS prototypes for the ALICE 3 vertex detector"_**"

    ???+ abstract
    	A major upgrade of the ALICE experiment is foreseen for Run 5 of the LHC. The new retractable Vertex Detector (VD) is a crucial component of the upgraded experiment and will achieve unprecedented pointing resolution through its close proximity to the interaction point, with three layers installed inside the beam pipe, in combination with a low material budget. Monolithic active pixel sensors (MAPS) are ideally suited to this detector, but require significant research and development to meet the combined requirements for position and time resolution and radiation hardness while limiting power consumption. In addition, the desired position resolution of 2.5 $\mu m$, and thus a pixel pitch of 10 $\mu m$, requires unprecedented integration density.
    	
    	This contribution presents radiation-induced leakage and voltage-dependent operating limits in irradiated APTS prototypes. The operating range is evaluated using VOUT&ndash;VRESET transfer curves and pixel-baseline stability as functions of temperature, reset current (IReset) and reverse substrate bias (VBB). For devices irradiated to $1 \times 10^{16}$ 1-MeV $n_{\rm eq}/{\rm cm}^2$, increasing IReset from 100 pA to 200 pA raises the measured operating-temperature limit from approximately $-0.5\ {}^{\rm o}$C to $4–10\ {}^{\rm o}$C, depending on VBB. The limit changes little between VBB values of 1.2 V and 4.8 V, and no strong dependence of the inferred leakage current on reverse bias is observed. Leakage current is estimated indirectly from the reset circuit failure; within uncertainties, this estimate agrees with an extrapolation from previous measurements on 15-$\mu m$-pitch APTS prototypes. These results help obtain leakage-related operating conditions and motivate direct current measurements with improved chip-temperature control.

#### 28.10 - Christopher Klumm

!!! note "Title: **_"Efficiency and DCA Studies for Pions from $\pmb{b}$-Quarks, and $\pmb{B^0}$ Reconstruction with ALICE 3"_**"

    ???+ abstract
        This study evaluates ${\rm B}^0$-meson reconstruction within the proposed ALICE 3 detector concept (reduced v3b layout) using the ACTS framework and KFParticle package. First, the tracking performance for pions from ${\rm B}$-hadron decays was evaluated, with iterative tracking achieving $> 90$% efficiency for $p_{\rm T} > 200\ \rm{MeV}$/$c$ and showing only moderate degradation under in-bunch pileup. Next, Distance of Closest Approach (DCA) studies investigate the DCAz for track-to-primary-vertex association and the DCAxy for discriminating primary from displaced secondary tracks. Applying a mass-constrained fit on intermediate $\rm{D}^⁻$ candidates, the ${\rm B}^0 \rightarrow {\rm D}^- + \pi^+ \rightarrow ({\rm K}^+ + 2\pi^-) + \pi^+$ decay chain is successfully reconstructed close to its nominal mass. The dominant correlated background $({\rm B}^0 → {\rm D}^{*-} + \pi^+)$ yields a characteristic broadened, downward-shifted mass spectrum due to the unmeasured $\pi^0$. These results confirm the feasibility of ${\rm B}^0$ signal reconstruction while characterizing the main background contribution for the proposed ALICE 3 detector concept.
        
    !!! success "Cake: Moric"

#### 04.11 - Luis Förschler, Berkin Ulukutlu

!!! note "Title: "**_"HYPER experiment at CERN AD"_**"

    ???+ abstract
    	While nucleon&ndash;nucleon interactions are well established, hyperon&ndash;nucleon (YN) and hyperon&ndash;nucleon&ndash;nucleon (YNN) interactions remain poorly constrained experimentally. Hypernuclei offer a unique opportunity to probe these interactions and extend our understanding of the strong interaction beyond ordinary nuclear matter. HYPER [[1]](https://cds.cern.ch/record/2931010/files/SPSC-I-263.pdf) aims to address this by exploiting low-energy antiproton annihilation on nuclear targets to produce heavy, neutron-rich hypernuclei, including systems difficult to access with conventional kaon or pion beams.
    
    	The hypernuclear binding energies will be reconstructed from two-body decay kinematics by measuring both the decay pion and the recoiling ion. While the latter will be identified using a dedicated Timepix-based hybrid silicon detector, the decay pions will be tracked with an ultra-low-material-budget CMOS MAPS tracker ($< 0.1\%\ X_{0}$ per layer), based on the MOSAIX chip developed for the ALICE ITS3 upgrade. Its fine pixel pitch of ~$20\ \mu m$ is expected to enable a binding-energy resolution of ~500 keV. In addition, a downstream time-of-flight layer will use the characteristic delay of weak hyperon decays to distinguish strangeness-producing events from prompt annihilation channels.
    
    	The talk will introduce HYPER's physics objectives and experimental concept, with particular emphasis on the CMOS MAPS tracker currently under development at TUM.
    
    	[1]  [HYPER Letter of Intent (LOI), [CERN-SPSC-I-263]](https://cds.cern.ch/record/2931010/files/SPSC-I-263.pdf).

#### 11.11 - Chiara Bellenghi (invited talk)

!!! note "Title: **_"Seeing the Universe in a Cubic Kilometer of Ice"_**"

    ???+ abstract
    	Two kilometers deep in the Antarctic ice, the more than 5,000 light sensors of the IceCube Neutrino Observatory have been detecting faint flashes of blue light for over 15 years. These flashes are Cherenkov radiation from charged particles produced when a TeV&ndash;PeV neutrino interacts with a nucleus in the ice. Such interactions are rare: neutrinos interact so weakly that a cubic kilometer of detection medium is needed to detect them. Yet their elusiveness is what makes them ideal cosmic messengers. Produced when cosmic-ray protons interact with matter and radiation near their accelerators, neutrinos travel unimpeded from their sources. They reach us even from the densest environments in the Universe, regions that light cannot escape, pointing back to their origin.
    	
    	The 2026 Nobel Prize in Physics, awarded to Francis Halzen, recognized the birth of a new kind of astronomy made possible by IceCube. The first breakthrough was the detection of a diffuse flux of high-energy neutrinos of astrophysical origin in 2013; today, the observatory is beginning to identify galaxy cores as its sources. In this seminar, I will review IceCube's key results in the quest for cosmic accelerators and conclude with an outlook on the near future of neutrino astronomy as next-generation neutrino telescopes begin to complement IceCube's view of the sky.
    
    !!! success "Cake: Anton and Daniel"
