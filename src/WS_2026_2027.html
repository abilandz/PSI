    21.10 - Marta Sailer

    Title: "Radiation-induced leakage and operating-temperature limits in APTS prototypes for the ALICE 3 vertex detector"

    Abstract

    A major upgrade of the ALICE experiment is foreseen for Run 5 of the LHC. The new retractable Vertex Detector (VD) is a crucial component of the upgraded experiment and will achieve unprecedented pointing resolution through its close proximity to the interaction point, with three layers installed inside the beam pipe, in combination with a low material budget. Monolithic active pixel sensors (MAPS) are ideally suited to this detector, but require significant research and development to meet the combined requirements for position and time resolution and radiation hardness while limiting power consumption. In addition, the desired position resolution of 2.5 \(\mu m\), and thus a pixel pitch of 10 \(\mu m\), requires unprecedented integration density.

    This contribution presents radiation-induced leakage and voltage-dependent operating limits in irradiated APTS prototypes. The operating range is evaluated using VOUT–VRESET transfer curves and pixel-baseline stability as functions of temperature, reset current (IReset) and reverse substrate bias (VBB). For devices irradiated to \(1 \times 10^{16}\) 1-MeV \(n_{\rm eq}/{\rm cm}^2\), increasing IReset from 100 pA to 200 pA raises the measured operating-temperature limit from approximately \(-0.5\ {}^{\rm o}\)C to \(4–10\ {}^{\rm o}\)C, depending on VBB. The limit changes little between VBB values of 1.2 V and 4.8 V, and no strong dependence of the inferred leakage current on reverse bias is observed. Leakage current is estimated indirectly from the reset circuit failure; within uncertainties, this estimate agrees with an extrapolation from previous measurements on 15-\(\mu m\)-pitch APTS prototypes. These results help obtain leakage-related operating conditions and motivate direct current measurements with improved chip-temperature control.

    28.10 - Christopher Klumm

    Title: "Efficiency and DCA Studies for Pions from \(\pmb{b}\)-Quarks, and \(\pmb{B^0}\) Reconstruction with ALICE 3"

    Abstract

    This study evaluates \({\rm B}^0\)-meson reconstruction within the proposed ALICE 3 detector concept (reduced v3b layout) using the ACTS framework and KFParticle package. First, the tracking performance for pions from \({\rm B}\)-hadron decays was evaluated, with iterative tracking achieving \(> 90\)% efficiency for \(p_{\rm T} > 200\ \rm{MeV}\)/\(c\) and showing only moderate degradation under in-bunch pileup. Next, Distance of Closest Approach (DCA) studies investigate the DCAz for track-to-primary-vertex association and the DCAxy for discriminating primary from displaced secondary tracks. Applying a mass-constrained fit on intermediate \(\rm{D}^⁻\) candidates, the \({\rm B}^0 \rightarrow {\rm D}^- + \pi^+ \rightarrow ({\rm K}^+ + 2\pi^-) + \pi^+\) decay chain is successfully reconstructed close to its nominal mass. The dominant correlated background \(({\rm B}^0 → {\rm D}^{*-} + \pi^+)\) yields a characteristic broadened, downward-shifted mass spectrum due to the unmeasured \(\pi^0\). These results confirm the feasibility of \({\rm B}^0\) signal reconstruction while characterizing the main background contribution for the proposed ALICE 3 detector concept.
