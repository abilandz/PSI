#### 29.10 - Wenya Wu

!!! note "Title: **_"Flow in small systems"_**"

    ???+ abstract
    	Quantum Chromodynamics predicts a transition from hadronic matter to a quark&ndash;gluon plasma (QGP) at extreme temperature and energy density, where quarks and gluons become deconfined. The QGP can be produced in high-energy heavy-ion collisions (HICs). Anisotropic flow coefficients, which characterize the collective motion of the medium, serve as key signatures of QGP formation. Measurements of transverse differential anisotropic flow reveal a baryon–meson grouping at intermediate transverse momentum, confirming QGP formation in HICs. Surprisingly, similar QGP-like features have been observed in high-multiplicity proton&ndash;proton and proton&ndash;lead collisions at the Large Hadron Collider (LHC). The ALICE Collaboration has reported, for the first time, a clear baryon&ndash;meson flow grouping in these small systems. Hydrodynamic models with quark coalescence successfully describe these results, suggesting that collective partonic behavior emerge in small collision systems.

#### 12.11 - Sebastian Wind, Seyed Farid Taghavi

!!! note "Title: **_"Collective Dynamics in OO and pp Collision Systems"_**"

    ???+ abstract
    	In the first part, we discuss how collective behavior in high-energy collisions can be understood using a kinetic quasiparticle approach based on the Boltzmann equation. We introduce CoMBolt-ITA, a new tool for simulating relativistic systems in the relaxation time approximation, and apply it to recent OO collision data at the LHC to assess whether the system behaves more fluid-like or particle-like. In the second part, we compare source size predictions in pp collisions for free-streaming and ideal hydrodynamic evolution to highlight how spatial correlations reveal the degree of collectivity in small systems.
    
    !!! success "Cake: Mrunmoy"

#### 19.11 - Maximilian Mahlein

!!! note "Title: **_"First observation of (anti)deuteron formation from resonance-decay nucleons"_**"

    ???+ abstract
    	The microscopic mechanism responsible for the formation of light (anti)(hyper)nuclei in hadron&ndash;hadron collisions remains one of the open questions in high-energy nuclear physics. While statistical hadronization and coalescence models both reproduce the measured yields, momentum spectra, and fluctuations in pp, p&ndash;A, and A&ndash;A collisions at ultra-relativistic energies, their fundamental physical assumptions are different, leading to model-dependent interpretations of the data.
    	
    	In this work, new femtoscopy results on pion&ndash;deuteron correlations in pp collisions at $\sqrt{s} = 13$ TeV, measured by the ALICE experiment, are presented. The correlation function directly probes the residual interaction between pions and nucleons originating from short-lived $\Delta$ decays, followed by their coalescence into deuterons. This measurement provides, for the first time, a model-independent demonstration that (anti)deuteron production in ultra-relativistic pp collisions proceeds via nucleon coalescence. The results offer new constraints for the modeling of light- and heavy-nuclei formation in cosmic ray interactions and dark matter decay scenarios.
    
    !!! success "Cake: Anton"

#### 26.11 - Philipp Mann, Berkin Ulukutlu

!!! note "Title: **_"Investigations with Bent BabyMOSS"_**"

    ???+ abstract
    	The ALICE experiment at CERN is upgrading its Inner Tracking System (ITS) as part of the ITS3 project, which will replace the innermost tracking layers with wafer-scale, cylindrically bent Monolithic Active Pixel Sensors (MAPS). These new layers are placed closer to the collision point and have a lower material budget, leading to improved vertexing resolution. The use of bent silicon detectors at this scale is unprecedented and requires extensive R&D to address challenges related to the sensor geometry. In particular, it is important to understand how bending affects sensor behavior and performance. Earlier bent-chip studies focused on the ALPIDE sensor from ITS2 and the APTS chiplets from the MLR1 production of ITS3. In this talk, we present new bent-sensor studies conducted with the BabyMOSS chip, a 0.7-megapixel fully functional prototype fabricated using the 65 nm process. We describe the sensor design, the experimental setup developed for the bent-chip measurements, and provide an overview of results from laboratory and test beam studies, where a significant shift in the chip’s operating point was observed.
    
    !!! success "Cake: Jossy"

#### 10.12 - Tobias Jenegger

!!! note "Title: **_"Advancements in Analysis & Instrumentation @ R3B"_**"

    ???+ abstract
    	The R3B (Reactions with Relativistic Radioactive ion Beams) experiment, a key component of the NUSTAR collaboration at FAIR, enables high-precision investigations of radioactive nuclei in inverse kinematics through its large acceptance and complete kinematic reconstruction. As such it offers the unique possibility for understanding the dynamics of nuclear reactions under extreme conditions. This contribution presents recent progress in two central analysis fields: (i) benchmark measurements of the total interaction cross section of 12C + 12C for equation of-state studies at R3B, and (ii) quasi-free scattering reactions, including the pilot experiment fission via quasi-free scatttering ${}^{238}U(p, 2p)$ fission.
    	
    	In addition, recent instrumental developments are highlighted, with a particular focus on the CALIFA calorimeter. The TUM group plays a leading role in its development and operation, contributing to hardware upgrades, software improvements, and the application of machine-learning techniques for enhanced cluster reconstruction. All these advancements contribute significantly to the promising experimental campaigns planned for the Early Science program at FAIR, scheduled to begin in 2028.
    
    !!! success "Cake: Georgios"

#### 17.12 - Marta Krawczyk

!!! note "Title: **_"Study of Monolithic Active Pixel Sensors for ALICE 3"_**"

    ???+ abstract
    	The ALICE collaboration plans to replace its experimental apparatus for the LHC Run 5 by an entirely new one, called ALICE 3. A key element of ALICE 3 will be the Vertex Detector (VD). To achieve the optimal pointing resolution, the detector will combine a $10\mu$m pixel pitch and a small distance of only 5 mm to the interaction point with minimal material budget. Furthermore, due to the proximity to the beam pipe as well as the expected interaction rate of about 25 MHz in proton-proton collisions, the pixel chips of the VD will need to resist a radiation load of approximately $10^{16}$ 1 MeV $n_{\rm eq}/{\rm cm}^2$ of Non-Ionizing Energy Loss (NIEL) and approximately 300 Mrad of Total-Ionizing Dose (TID). Moreover, a time resolution of 100 ns is targeted. These requirements constitute a significant challenge to CMOS pixel sensors planned to be employed for the VD.
    	
    	This seminar talk describes the recently started pixel chip characterization work at the expected radiation levels and as a function of temperature using the Analog Pixel Test Structure (APTS). The key items of this presentation will be the temperature and radiation dependence of the gain and the operation range of the APTS circuitry.
    
    !!! success "Cake: Lukas"

#### 07.01 - Georgios Mantzaridis

!!! note "Title: **_"Probing the strangeness -2 sector with femtoscopy in ALICE with Run 3 data"_**"

    ???+ abstract
    	The interaction between nucleons and hyperons in the double-strange sector play a critical role in determining the equation of state of dense baryonic matter, with implications for the structure of neutron stars. Traditionally, this sector has been studied by using hypernuclear spectroscopy. However, the scarce data on double strange hypernuclei have so far limited the extraction of precise information on the ΞN interaction and the nature of the hyothetical (quasi)boundstate in the ΛΛ system is still not fully setteled.
    	
    	During the LHC Run 2 data taking, the ALICE Collaboration has provided rich information on the low-energy features of the Λ-Λ and p–Ξ− interactions by using the femtoscopy technique. The results were able to constrain (but not close) the parameterspace for the boundstate nature of in the ΛΛ and benchmark for the first time Lattice QCD calculations in the p–Ξ− system, providing experimental evidence of the attractive nature of the ΞN strong interaction. With the start of the LHC Run 3 data taking in 2022, ALICE has begun collecting significantly larger datasets, providing evidence of a pronounced signal at the opening of the Λ–Σ0 channel in the p–Ξ− correlation function. In this seminar we will discuss the current femtoscopic results in the strangeness -2 sector and present the preliminary result on p–Ξ− correlations measured in pp collisions from the first three years of the LHC Run 3. The measurement will be discussed by comparing the data to the correlation function calculated using the HAL-QCD potentials. #### 14.01 - Mrunmoy Jena

!!! note "Title: **_"Analyzing (p,2p) and (p,pn) quasi-free scattering at the R3B Experiment"_**"

    ???+ abstract
    	Quasi-free (proton and neutron knockout) reactions in inverse kinematics provide a valuable insight into the structure of nuclei far from stability. Providing kinematically complete measurements for incoming nuclei and outgoing reaction products, the R3B setup (Reactions with Relativistic Radioactive Beams) is ideal for studying such reactions. 
    	
    	Recently, the quasi-free scattering reaction ${}^{25}F(p,2p){}^{24}O$ has been measured with this setup in an attempt to understand the extension of the neutron drip line from $Z=8$ nuclei to $Z=9$ nuclei. This talk would highlight ongoing analysis of this experiment and energy reconstruction by the CALIFA calorimeter. In addition to this, approaches to identify neutron clusters in CALIFA, using Monte Carlo simulations will be discussed.

#### 21.01 - Rafael Manhart

!!! note "Title: **_"Results of the latest femtoscopic studies involving pions by ALICE in Run 3 & the connection to NS and axions"_**"

    ???+ abstract
        The composition of the innermost region of neutron stars remains unknown, and the potential presence of QCD axions has been recently suggested. The properties of axions under high baryonic densities can be related to the in medium characteristics of pions, which can be studied in pp collisions at the LHC. The emission of multiple hadrons helps mimic high densities because their production distances are short (∼ fm). This talk presents the latest ALICE Run 3 measurements of the pion-proton interaction using femtoscopy in pp collisions at $\sqrt{s} = 13.6$ TeV. Using three-body femtoscopy, we report the first measurement of p&ndash;p&ndash;$\pi$ correlations in the ongoing Run 3 data-taking period, enabling the study of effects beyond pairwise interactions. This offers a distinctive opportunity to study axions and their impact on the equation of state of neutron stars.
    
    !!! success "Cake: Wenya"

#### 28.01 - Maximilian Korwieser

!!! note "Title: **_"Fast Femtoscopic Source Modeling with Machine Learning: From Collisions to Cosmic Rays"_**"

    ???+ abstract
        Femtoscopic correlation measurements provide direct access to the space&ndash;time structure of particle emission in high-energy collisions. However, quantitative source studies are often limited by the high computational cost of evaluating realistic source models, making large-scale scans and systematic studies impractical. In this talk, I present a machine-learning surrogate model for femtoscopic sources that accurately emulates the full model calculation while reducing the evaluation time to effectively constant cost. Once trained, the surrogate enables fast and robust fits to arbitrarily large sets of correlation functions, opening new possibilities for high-statistics and multi-system analyses. Beyond acceleration, I discuss a task-transfer application: using the surrogate trained on heavy-ion data to estimate femtoscopic sources in very low-multiplicity and low-energy environments, where direct femtoscopic measurements are scarce. Machine learning is ideally suited to provide data-driven estimates in this regime. Such systems are relevant for understanding (anti-)nuclei production in cosmic rays, providing essential input for indirect dark matter searches by AMS and GAPS.
    
    !!! success "Cake: Ante"

#### 04.02 - Claudia Manunza, Giulia Pruna, Lukas Lautner

!!! note "Title: **_"ALICE 3 Outer Tracker Cooling Studies"_**"

    ???+ abstract
        The ALICE collaboration has proposed a completely new apparatus, ALICE 3, for the LHC Run 5. The detector consists of a large tracking system based on Monolithic Active Pixel Sensors (MAPS), complemented by multiple systems for particle identification. The tracking system is divided into Inner and Outer Tracker. With $32m^2$, just the four barrel layers of the Outer Tracker will constitute the largest MAPS-based detector ever built and will require modularization and industrialization of its production. The about 45000 individual sensors will combined consume more than 10 kW and require dedicated cooling. Keeping modularity in mind, air cooling is the preferred option. Ongoing R&D work is focused on identifying the optimal cooling solution for the Outer Tracker. For this purpose, a cooling setup was build and recently upgraded at TUM. To adequately simulate the heat load dummy heater modules are employed and continuously developed to more closely match operational conditions.
        
        We report on the upgrade of the setup, studied cooling methods and present first results showing the feasibility of air cooling the Outer Tracker.
    
    !!! success "Cake: Wioleta"
