    Mrunmoy Jena

    Title: "Spectroscopy of ²⁴O and ²²O via ²⁵F(p,2p) quasi-free scattering at R3B Experiment""

    Abstract

    Quasi-free proton and neutron-knockout reactions in inverse kinematics offer valuable insight into the structure of nuclei far from stability, and the kinematically complete R3B setup (Reactions with Relativistic Radioactive Beams) is ideally suited to study these reactions. The ²⁵F(p,2p)²⁴O reaction probes how a single proton extends the neutron drip line from Z=8, where it ends at ²⁴O, to Z=9 — where the ground-state overlap of ²⁵F with ²⁴O turns out to be surprisingly small, and its structural origin remains debated. Since the excited states of ²⁴O are neutron-unbound, they are reconstructed through their ²³O + n and ²²O + 2n decay channels.

    This talk presents the ongoing analysis, focusing on energy reconstruction with the CALIFA calorimeter and, in particular, on the ²²O + 2n + γ channel. From Doppler-corrected γ-ray spectra, the populations of the low-lying ²²O states are extracted using simulated response functions, and a γ-multiplicity analysis is developed to separate direct population of the 2⁺ state from cascade feeding.

    Lena Kirchner, Anil Adigüzel

    Title: "(baby)HYPER"

    Abstract

    HYPER (Single-λ Hypernuclei Factory) is a pioneering experiment at CERN’s Antimatter Factory designed to produce and discover approximately 200 new single-λ hypernuclei using low-energy antiprotons from the ELENA ring. By capturing antiprotons onto nuclei, HYPER aims to perform high-resolution spectroscopy to measure ground-state binding energies and lifetimes, providing critical data to constrain λ-nucleon and λNN interactions and address the "hyperon puzzle" in neutron star physics. The experiment relies on a compact, multi-purpose detector — utilizing advanced ALICE ITS3 65 nm CMOS technology for its pion tracker — capable of distinguishing weak-decay events from prompt annihilation backgrounds through picosecond timing.

    The second part of the talk is about babyHYPER, a dedicated testbed operated during a test beam campaign at the TELMAX beamline at AD. It was designed to prototype key technical solutions for HYPER and to study particle tracking with MAPS detectors in a realistic setup. The testbed consists of a vacuum vessel with vacuum separation and two detector arms, each equipped with three sensors. This configuration allows the performance of the cooling and vacuum systems to be investigated while providing tracking information for vertex reconstruction at the target. The presentation will discuss the vessel design, the role of vacuum separation, and results from sensor cooling, vacuum performance, and tracking analysis.

    24.06.2026

    Henrik Fribert

    Title: "New femtoscopic constraints on the ΣN interaction from pp collisions at √s = 13.6 TeV with ALICE"

    Abstract

    The properties of dense astrophysical objects, such as neutron stars, are governed by the equation of state of nuclear matter. At the extreme baryon densities reached in their cores, hyperons are expected to appear as energetically favorable degrees of freedom. Constraining hyperon-nucleon interactions is therefore essential for accurate astrophysical modeling. However, in the case of the ΣN interaction, the experimental information is scarce.

    In this talk, unprecedented precision results on Σ⁻p correlations measured by ALICE in pp collisions at √s = 13.6 TeV during the LHC Run 3 are presented. These measurements are made possible thanks to the excellent tracking capabilities of the new Inner Tracking System, which allows the reconstruction of charged Σ hyperons via their decay to a neutral particle and a charged daughter, identified by the characteristic kink topology of the decay.

    The measured correlation functions provide direct access to the strong interaction effects, enabling comparisons with different theoretical interaction potentials. These new results complement the previous Σ⁺p femtoscopic measurements performed by ALICE in Run 2, and pave the way for improved constraints on the strength and isospin dependence of the ΣN interaction, offering essential insights for the equation of state of dense nuclear matter.

    Davide Cremese

    Title: "Probing the initial-state geometry with the Lee-Yang zeros"

    Abstract

    In relativistic heavy-ion collisions, anisotropic flow provides insights into the collective behavior of the quark-gluon plasma. The Lee-Yang zero method provides a robust framework for anisotropic flow analysis, suppressing non-flow effects through the interparticle correlation between a large number of particles. While this method utilizes the first zero of a generating function in order to isolate collective flow effects, the information that additional zeros may hold remains unexplored. This talk focuses on the application of the second Lee-Yang zero as a complementary observable in the context of flow measurements. Using simulated Pb-Pb collision data generated with the Trento model, we analyze its sensitivity to initial-state parameters and compare its performance to the standard first-zero approach and the established multi-particle correlation method.

    10.06.2026 Rafael Manhart: "ALICE femtoscopic studies involving pions in Run 2/3 & the connection to NS and axions"

    Abstract: The composition of the innermost region of neutron stars remains unknown, and the potential presence of QCD axions has been recently suggested. The properties of axions under high baryonic densities can be related to the in-medium characteristics of pions, which can be studied in pp collisions at the LHC. The emission of multiple hadrons helps mimic high densities because their production distances are short (∼ fm). This talk presents ALICE Run 2 & Run 3 measurements of the pion-proton interaction using femtoscopy in pp collisions at √s = 13 TeV & √s = 13.6 TeV. Using three-body femtoscopy, we report the first measurement of p-p-pi correlations in the ongoing Run 3 data-taking period, enabling the study of effects beyond pairwise interactions. This offers a distinctive opportunity to study axions and their impact on the equation of state of neutron stars.

    03.06.2026 Lukas Lautner, Philipp Mann: "ALICE 3 Outer Tracker Cooling Studies"

    Abstract: The ALICE collaboration has proposed a completely new apparatus, ALICE 3, for the LHC Run 5. The detector consists of a large tracking system based on Monolithic Active Pixel Sensors (MAPS), complemented by multiple systems for particle identification. The tracking system is divided into Inner and Outer Tracker. With 32m2, just the four barrel layers of the Outer Tracker will constitute the largest MAPS-based detector ever built and will require modularization and industrialization of its production. The combined power consumption of the roughly 45000 individual sensors will exceed 10 kW and require dedicated cooling. Keeping modularity in mind, air cooling is the preferred option. Ongoing R&D work is focused on identifying the optimal cooling solution for the Outer Tracker. For this purpose, we built and continuously upgrade a cooling setup at TUM. To adequately simulate the heat load dummy heater modules are employed and continuously developed to more closely match operational conditions.

    We report on the upgrade of the setup, present results showing the feasibility of air cooling the Outer Tracker Barrel and first estimates for the required services.

    27.05.2026 Wioleta Rzesa: "Probing three-body dynamics of p–p–Λ via correlation functions in ALICE at the LHC"

    Abstract: The presence of hyperons in dense stellar matter is expected to soften the equation of state, giving rise to the so-called hyperon puzzle of neutron stars. Traditional calculations that lead to this conclusion rely primarily on two-body interactions when modeling hyperonic matter. Attempts to include the missing three-body contributions — based on available experimental constraints from Λ-hypernuclei — are limited by the insufficient precision of current data relative to theoretical developments. High-precision femtoscopic measurements can therefore substantially improve the description of the hyperon–nucleon–nucleon systems. During Run 2 at the Large Hadron Collider (LHC), the ALICE experiment reported the first measurement of femtoscopic correlations among p–p–Λ triplets produced in pp collisions. In this contribution, the preliminary Run 3 results for p–p–Λ correlations measured by ALICE in Pb–Pb collisions are presented. Comparing these measurements enables a differential characterization of the dynamics for different emission environments and to study the extent to which three-body forces are encoded in the p–p–Λ correlation function.

    20.05.2026 Sergei Golenev: "HI-TREX: Compact, high resolution particle detection system for ISOLDE"

    Abstract: HI-TREX is an advanced particle detection system designed for the HIE-ISOLDE facility at CERN, optimized for studying transfer reactions with radioactive ion beams. The system features thin double-sided silicon strip detectors (DSSSDs), high-resolution front-end electronics based on SKIROC ASICs, and a custom FPGA-based readout board integrated into the TRB data acquisition system. The detector performance has been evaluated in dedicated beam tests, including a recent experiment at the Cologne tandem accelerator with different targets and both proton and 16O beams. I will present the first data, highlighting the enhanced capabilities of HI-TREX and its potential to enable new research opportunities.

    06.05.2026 Elias Sevdas: "Proton Tracking for Quasi-Free Scattering Reactions with Pixel Detectors at R3B"

    Abstract: The structure of exotic nuclei can be investigated through $\((p, 2p)\)$ quasi-free scattering in inverse kinematics, in which a proton is knocked out of the projectile nucleus by a target proton. The R3B experiment (Reactions with Relativistic Radioactive Beams) allows for a kinematically complete reconstruction of these reactions. A key observable is the excitation energy ($\(E_x\)$) of the fragment. It is calculated from the beam information, the fragment mass, and the reconstructed momenta of the two outgoing protons.

    For a precise measurement of $\(E_x\)$, an accurate determination of the momentum vectors $\(\vec{p}\)$ for both outgoing protons is essential. The proton trajectories will be measured using a new Target Recoil Tracker (TRT) in a two-barrel configuration with a barrel thickness of approximately 565 $\(\textmu m\)$. It is being considered to extend this setup with a third, significantly thinner 50 $\(\textmu m\)$ silicon barrel at the innermost position. The motivation is to improve the excitation energy resolution and to filter out the delta-electron background arising from the heavy ions traversing the target. This thesis investigates these improvements using GEANT4-based Monte Carlo simulations in the R3BRoot framework.

    To study the delta-electron background, a simulation was performed with $\({}^{238}\rm{U}\)$ ions at $\(E_{\rm kin} = 400\)$ MeV/u. The fraction of unresolvable tracks in both barrel geometries was quantified. While in the two-barrel configuration 57% of all proton tracks could not be resolved from contamination by delta-electrons, two new methods developed in the framework of this thesis for the three-barrel configuration reduced this background contribution to 3.9%.

    Regarding the excitation energy resolution, it was shown analytically and confirmed in simulations that among the reconstructed vertex coordinates, the uncertainty along the beam axis is the dominant contribution. A $\((p, 2p)\)$ reaction with $\({}^{132}\rm{Sn}\)$ at an energy of $\(E_{\rm kin} = 400\)$ MeV/u was simulated for both configurations, and the excitation energy resolution was quantified. Because the three-barrel setup resolves the coordinate along the beam axis more precisely than the two-barrel setup, the full width at half maximum (FWHM) excitation energy resolution improves from $\(\Delta E_x = 3.91\)$ MeV to $\(\Delta E_x = 2.83\)$ MeV.

    As a potential future improvement, a foil stack target was investigated to reduce the vertex uncertainty along the beam axis, improving the excitation energy resolution to $\(\Delta E_x = 0.94\)$ MeV (FWHM) with the three-barrel setup.

    29.04.2026 Maximilian Korwieser: "Normalizing-flow surrogate for femtoscopic source functions"

    Abstract: Precise predictions of cosmic-ray antideuteron fluxes, a prime dark matter signature owing to their exceptionally low astrophysical background, require fully data-constrained femtoscopic source functions as input to state-of-the-art Wigner-function coalescence calculations, superseding the simplistic spherical momentum cut-off approach. Such sources remain poorly constrained, particularly in the ultra-low multiplicity regime most relevant for astrophysical applications. We present a normalizing-flow surrogate for femtoscopic source functions, trained on CECA (Common Emission in CATS, a state-of-the-art effective source model for small collision systems which encapsulates the effects of resonance production) simulations and simultaneously fit to 49 correlation functions double-differential in pair transverse mass mT and event multiplicity. Exploiting the established mT-multiplicity scaling of the emission source, higher-statistics event classes directly constrain the poorly populated low-multiplicity domain, delivering fully data-driven source functions without free parameters in the extrapolation. The surrogate is fast, differentiable, and accurate: closure tests confirm sub-percent emulation fidelity, and physics-driven validation recovers the correct mT scaling and multiplicity dependence across all event classes. The resulting source-constrained B2 predictions are directly applicable to ongoing low-energy measurements at SMOG@LHCb, CBM@FAIR, and NA61/SHINE, and the framework is fully modular: any source model can be substituted or our B2 output ingested directly, with cosmic-ray propagation via standard transport codes the sole remaining step toward absolute antideuteron flux predictions for AMS-02 and GAPS.
