**Abstract:** 

Precise predictions of cosmic-ray antideuteron fluxes, a prime dark matter signature owing to their exceptionally low astrophysical background, require fully data-constrained femtoscopic source functions as input to state-of-the-art Wigner-function coalescence calculations, superseding the simplistic spherical momentum cut-off approach. Such sources remain poorly constrained, particularly in the ultra-low multiplicity regime most relevant for astrophysical applications. We present a normalizing-flow surrogate for femtoscopic source functions, trained on CECA (Common Emission in CATS, a state-of-the-art effective source model for small collision systems which encapsulates the effects of resonance production) simulations and simultaneously fit to 49 correlation functions double-differential in pair transverse mass mT and event multiplicity. Exploiting the established mT-multiplicity scaling of the emission source, higher-statistics event classes directly constrain the poorly populated low-multiplicity domain, delivering fully data-driven source functions without free parameters in the extrapolation. The surrogate is fast, differentiable, and accurate: closure tests confirm sub-percent emulation fidelity, and physics-driven validation recovers the correct mT scaling and multiplicity dependence across all event classes. The resulting source-constrained B2 predictions are directly applicable to ongoing low-energy measurements at SMOG@LHCb, CBM@FAIR, and NA61/SHINE, and the framework is fully modular: any source model can be substituted or our B2 output ingested directly, with cosmic-ray propagation via standard transport codes the sole remaining step toward absolute antideuteron flux predictions for AMS-02 and GAPS.
